| 1 | Zeineddine, H.; Braendle, U.; Farah, A. | 2021 | Applied **Automated Machine Learning (AutoML)** to automatically search and select the best classifier (from Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVM), using only data available *before* the student even enrolls | Private university admission/pre-enrollment records (institution and exact sample size not disclosed in the paper) | Enables intervention before a program even starts; empirically shows AutoML beats manually-tuned ML on the same data | Single-institution dataset — authors explicitly caution the model may not transfer to other universities | No universal "best" algorithm exists across institutions — a model tuned for one university's data isn't guaranteed to work for another | AutoML-selected pipelines outperformed manually tuned individual classifiers for early, pre-enrollment prediction of student success | **114** (Semantic Scholar) | https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compeleceng.2020.106903 |
| 2 | Garmpis, S.; Maragoudakis, M.; Garmpis, A. | 2022 | Used the free, open-source **KNIME Analytics AutoML** platform to auto-search classifiers (Decision Tree, Random Forest, Naive Bayes, SVM, boosting variants) for predicting time-to-course-completion | 23,687 "Computer Networking" module student records from a Greek university | Free/open-source, low-code — usable by university staff with no ML background; validated on 3 real courses | Tested only within one university system that was undergoing departmental mergers — limited external validity | Institutions lack accessible AutoML tooling for non-expert staff to flag at-risk / delayed-graduation students | AutoML (KNIME) correctly predicted ~92% of outcomes for students likely to drop out or delay graduation | Newly indexed at time of writing — check live Semantic Scholar count | https://doi.org/10.3390/computers11060097 |
| 3 | Tsiakmaki, M.; Kostopoulos, G.; Kotsiantis, S.; Ragos, O. | 2019 (pub. 2020) | Compared **Auto-WEKA / Auto-sklearn AutoML search** directly against manually-tuned classical ML (Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, SVM, kNN) across several distance-learning prediction tasks | Hellenic Open University distance-learning student records, across multiple courses/tasks | One of the earliest systematic AutoML-vs-manual-ML comparisons in Educational Data Mining; covers dropout, performance, and grade prediction together | Uses 2019-era AutoML tools (Auto-WEKA/Auto-sklearn), now dated relative to modern AutoML frameworks | No prior empirical evidence existed on whether AutoML could match/beat hand-tuned models specifically for student prediction tasks | AutoML matched or outperformed manually configured ML models across multiple EDM tasks, cutting the need for in-house ML expertise | Foundational, well-cited AutoML-in-EDM paper — check live Semantic Scholar count | https://doi.org/10.3390/app10010090 |
| 4 | Alsumaidaie, M.S.I.; Nafea, A.A.; Mukhlif, A.A.; Jalal, R.D.; AL-Ani, M.M. | 2024 | Compared three supervised classifiers — **Random Forest, Extra Trees, K-Nearest Neighbors** — following a full pipeline (data collection → preprocessing → feature identification → model construction → evaluation) | 30,000 student records (24,000 training / 6,000 testing) | Extra Trees delivered very high accuracy; KNN gave near-instant training time (~0.00 s), useful for real-time deployment | Exact dataset domain/source and precise precision/recall values aren't itemized numerically in the abstract | Existing models tend to trade off accuracy against computational efficiency — need one that is strong on both | Extra Trees achieved the highest accuracy (98.15%), ahead of Random Forest (94.03%) and KNN (91.65%) | Recently published (Nov 2024) — check live Semantic Scholar count | https://doi.org/10.21123/bsj.2024.9643 |

| 6 | Balachandar, V.; Venkatesh, K. | 2024 (pub. 2025) | Proposed **MSPP** — a deep-learning framework combining domain-specific preprocessing for imbalanced/temporal data, feature engineering, graph-neural-network layers, and an explainable-AI (XAI) output layer | Multi-class academic performance dataset (Distinction/Pass/Fail/Withdrawn categories, OULAD-style structure) | Explainable by design (XAI-integrated); handles multi-class imbalance well; clearly better at correctly flagging the hardest "Fail" class than 5 baseline models | Overall accuracy (76%) is moderate compared to some other studies; the added graph-neural-network layers increase computational cost | Traditional models struggle with multi-dimensional, imbalanced, temporal academic data and tend to give oversimplified, non-explainable outputs | MSPP outperformed 5 baseline models (EAI&ML, MTSDA, XAI, DGNN, DLM) — notably improving "Fail"-class precision to 0.66 (vs. 0.59 and 0.54) while cutting the false-positive rate to 0.15 | Recently published (Dec 2024/2025) — check live Semantic Scholar count | https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mex.2024.103148 |
| 7 | Sayed, A.F.A.; Arafa, M.A.; El-Nimr, N.A.; Banawan, K.A.S.; Abdou, M.S. | 2025 | Directly compared **classification** (Random Forest, XGBoost, SVC variants, CatBoost, kNN, Logistic Regression, Ensemble) against **regression** (ElasticNet, Ridge, RF, XGB, SVR variants, kNN, Ensemble Soft Voting), with grid-search tuning and 5-fold cross-validation | High Institute of Public Health, Alexandria University — 922 postgraduate records (2020–2024), 22 features, 75/25 split | Rigorous methodology (5-fold CV, grid search); rare direct head-to-head of regression vs. classification for the exact same academic-performance task | Recall stayed low even for the best classifier (0.53) — meaning many at-risk students would be missed in practice; data from a single institution | Most literature treats performance prediction only as classification; very few studies compare regression vs. classification on the same dataset, or include psychosocial/behavioral features | Regression's Ensemble (Soft Voting) beat the best classifier, achieving 74.25% accuracy vs. Random Forest's 71.43%; number of failed courses was the single strongest predictor (r = −0.37) | Newly published (Dec 2025) — check live Semantic Scholar count | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-31023-z |
| 8 | Bosch, N. | 2021 | Compared two **AutoML feature-engineering methods** — Featuretools and TSFRESH — against manually expert-engineered features, plus a novel feature-selection method built for very high-dimensional data (4,000+ features) | NAEP (National Assessment of Educational Progress) Data Mining Competition dataset — student interaction logs | Shows AutoML feature engineering (TSFRESH) can match or beat expert-engineered features with far less manual effort; the entry placed 1st on the competition's public leaderboard | AutoML-generated features (especially TSFRESH) were rated by experts as much harder to interpret than hand-crafted ones — a real accuracy/interpretability trade-off | No prior work had quantified whether automated feature engineering could match expert feature engineering specifically for large-scale student interaction-log data | TSFRESH-based AutoML features significantly outperformed both Featuretools and expert-engineered features (mean AUC 0.550 vs. 0.529 vs. 0.538); final competition AUC = 0.665 | Well-cited in the AutoML-in-education subfield — check live Semantic Scholar count | https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5275314 |
| 9 | Ahmed, E. | 2024 | Two-stage pipeline: **K-Means clustering** (unsupervised, exploratory) followed by tuned supervised classification/regression models, validated with repeated 10-fold cross-validation | **OULAD** (Open University Learning Analytics Dataset) — demographics, region, entrance result, prior attempts, studied credits, disability status | Combines clustering with prediction for actionable student grouping; built on a well-known public dataset, so results are fully reproducible | Best-model accuracy/precision/recall/F1 figures are not surfaced in the openly accessible abstract text — full PDF needed for exact numbers | E-learning providers need reproducible pipelines that combine clustering with prediction to actionably group at-risk students, not just report accuracy | Combining K-Means clustering with tuned supervised ML on OULAD improved the ability to segment and predict student performance groups compared to supervised-only pipelines | Recently published (Apr 2024) — check live Semantic Scholar count | https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/4067721 |
| 10 | Johora, F.T.; Hasan, M.N.; Rajbongshi, A.; Ashrafuzzaman, M.; Akter, F. | 2025 | Built a **stacking ensemble model** with SMOTE-based class-balancing (SMOTE-Tomek and SMOTE-ENN variants tested), and integrated **SHAP and LIME** (explainable AI) to make the model's predictions interpretable | Undergraduate academic performance dataset (demographic + academic records; exact institution/size detailed in-paper, not in abstract) | Directly tackles class imbalance (a common real-world problem); adds genuine explainability (SHAP + LIME) so predictions aren't a "black box" | Performance is sensitive to which balancing technique is used (SMOTE-Tomek vs. SMOTE-ENN gave different results) — adds a tuning burden | Most student-performance prediction models are accurate but not explainable, limiting trust and adoption by academic staff | Gradient Boosting with SMOTE-ENN achieved the best results: 85.31% accuracy, 85.51% precision, 85.30% recall, 85.31% F1-score | Newly published (Feb 2025) — check live Semantic Scholar count | https://doi.org/10.1016/j.array.2025.100384 |
